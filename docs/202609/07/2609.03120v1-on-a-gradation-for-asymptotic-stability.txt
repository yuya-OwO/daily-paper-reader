# On a Gradation for Asymptotic Stability

Abstract —Classical asymptotic stability guarantees conver-gence but does not quantify the rate at which convergence occurs. This paper introduces a gradation of asymptotic stability where degree zero corresponds to exponential stability and degree m > 

0 corresponds to algebraic decay of order t−1/m . We provide direct and converse Lyapunov tests for admissible degrees and conditions for certifying the exact stability degree. Hopf, Bautin, fractional-degree, and time-varying examples demonstrate how the degree identifies the leading stabilizing mechanism. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The stability of dynamical systems is a central topic in non-linear analysis, control theory, and applied mathematics [1]– [4]. The key notion of Lyapunov stability requires trajectories of a dynamical system to stay arbitrarily close to an equi-librium point if they start sufficiently close to it. This notion is further qualified as asymptotic stability when trajectories converge to the equilibrium point, and as exponential stability when this convergence satisfies an exponential bound. There is, however, a spectrum of bounds between convergence and exponential convergence. This gap is visible even in one of the most classical local bifurcation mechanisms, known as the Hopf bifurcation. Hopf bifurcations arise when a conjugate pair of eigenvalues crosses the imaginary axis and are a standard mechanism for the birth or disappearance of small-amplitude periodic orbits. The usual bifurcation-theoretic classification emphasizes the qualitative phase portrait: trajectories may spiral into an equilibrium, spi-ral away from it, or approach a stable or unstable limit cycle, depending on the signs of the unfolding parameter and the first Lyapunov coefficient [5]–[7]. However, this classification does not distinguish between different convergence laws within the attracting equilibrium regime. The intermediate algebraic regimes are therefore not explicitly organized by the usual qualitative terminology. 

Contributions. Motivated by this gap, we introduce a rate-based degree framework for local asymptotic stability. The framework is built from a canonical family of comparison envelopes: degree 0 corresponds to exponential convergence, while degree m > 0 corresponds to an admissible algebraic de-cay bound of order O(t−1/m ). We prove that degree estimates imply asymptotic stability and develop Lyapunov certificates for this gradation. In particular, an upper Lyapunov dissipation inequality certifies an admissible degree m, while a matching arXiv:2609.03120v1 [eess.SY] 2 Sep 2026 lower inequality on a forward-invariant set yields trajectories of order ≍ t−1/m , rules out every faster degree, and thereby certifies the exact degree. We also provide a nonsmooth converse characterization using upper Dini derivatives along solutions: a degree-m estimate is equivalent to the existence of a continuous Lyapunov certificate with matching degree-

m dissipation. Overall, the proposed framework complements classical Lyapunov stability theory by making decay rates explicit. II. R ELATED WORK 

Our work builds on classical Lyapunov stability theory and converse Lyapunov theorems. Uniform asymptotic stability can be characterized through class-KL trajectory estimates and Lyapunov functions; see, e.g., [3], [11], [12]. Converse results establish Lyapunov functions from such trajectory estimates in broad settings, including time-varying systems [13], [14]. These results provide general characterizations of asymptotic stability, whereas our objective is to introduce a one-parameter gradation that distinguishes asymptotically stable equilibria by their convergence rates. Subexponential convergence has also been studied through Lyapunov differential inequalities. Caraballo [15], for exam-ple, gives criteria for exponential, polynomial, logarithmic, and more general decay rates. Closely related is rational stability [16], [17], commonly expressed through an estimate of the form ∥ϕ(t, x )∥p ≤ M ∥x∥p/(1+ k∥x∥pt), where M, k > 0 and 

p is generally taken to be a positive integer. Such an estimate implies algebraic decay of order t−1/p . Our framework instead promotes the algebraic order to the stability parameter itself: degree m = 0 denotes exponential stability, while a real degree m > 0 corresponds to an admissible decay envelope of order t−1/m . We then identify the smallest admissible 

m, when attained, as the degree of the equilibrium, thereby organizing exponential and algebraic convergence within a single gradation. The recent work of Jagt and Peet [18] is particularly close to ours. They develop a framework for quantifying the rate performance of autonomous nonlinear ODEs relative to a prescribed family of trajectory bounds. In the rational case, this again takes the form ∥ϕ(t, x )∥p ≤ M ∥x∥p/(1 + k∥x∥pt):after the decay model and state exponent p are specified, 

k measures the rate and M the gain. More generally, their framework fixes a state measure and a comparison function and derives necessary and sufficient Lyapunov conditions for the associated rate and gain performance. Our framework addresses a different question: rather than fixing the algebraic decay order and quantifying the constants in the resulting estimate, we make that order itself the quantity to be classified. The single parameter m determines the family t−1/m , and the smallest admissible m, when attained, determines the degree of stability. Correspondingly, our Lyapunov characterization is indexed directly by m. Moreover, our framework and converse characterization apply directly to time-varying systems ˙x =

f (t, x ), whereas [18] develops its rate-performance framework for autonomous ODEs. Finally, the proposed degree is distinct from the classical Lyapunov exponent used to measure exponential growth or decay [19]. Since t−1 log ∥x(t)∥ → 0 for algebraically de-caying trajectories, different algebraic rates have the same zero Lyapunov exponent. Our degree refines this regime by distinguishing their algebraic decay orders. III. D EGREE OF STABILITY

Remark III.2 (Smooth scalar systems with finite leading Tay-lor order) . Smooth scalar autonomous systems with a finite nonzero Taylor leading term realize only even integer attained degrees. Indeed, suppose ˙x = f (x) = αx k + o(xk), α̸ = 0 ,near the origin. If the origin is locally asymptotically stable from both sides, then k must be odd and α < 0. If k = 1 , the origin is exponentially stable and has degree 0. If k > 1, the leading balance gives |x(t)| ≍ t−1/(k−1) , so D = [ k − 1, ∞)

and m = k − 1 ∈ D . Since k is odd, k − 1 is an even integer. This conclusion relies on the existence of a finite nonzero Taylor leading term. Smooth scalar systems that are flat at the origin are not covered by this argument and may exhibit non-polynomial or subalgebraic decay behavior. The observation is consistent with the classical homogeneous approximation principle: if the lowest-degree nonzero homogeneous com-ponent of a smooth vector field is asymptotically stable, then the full vector field is locally asymptotically stable [2], [20], [21]; asymptotically stable homogeneous systems admit homogeneous Lyapunov functions [22]. IV. L YAPUNOV CHARACTERIZATION 

We now turn from trajectory-based definitions to Lyapunov certificates. We first develop a direct Lyapunov method and illustrate it on normal forms, vector systems, and time-varying systems. We then establish a nonsmooth converse characteri-zation of admissible degree-m estimates.

Remark IV.5 (Smooth converse refinement) . Theorem IV.2 gives a continuous converse Lyapunov function. Under ad-ditional regularity assumptions on this value function, such as relative annular Lipschitz bounds, one can smooth the certificate while preserving the degree-m dissipation inequality up to controlled margins. We leave this smooth converse refinement as future work. V. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

We introduced a degree-based framework for local asymp-totic stability that places exponential and algebraic conver-gence rates on a common scale, with degree 0 corresponding to exponential convergence and degree m > 0 to algebraic decay of order t−1/m . We developed Lyapunov certificates for admissibility and exact degree stability, together with a nonsmooth converse characterization of uniform admissible degrees. The examples, including Hopf and Bautin normal forms, fractional-degree vector systems, and time-varying sys-tems, illustrate how the degree distinguishes different leading stabilizing mechanisms. Several directions remain for future work. First, we will develop Taylor-expansion-based algorithms for computing sta-bility degrees from the leading terms of smooth vector fields. The scalar case already suggests a simple classification by the first nonzero Taylor coefficient, while vector systems require handling homogeneous leading terms, mixed rates, center directions, and invariant slow sets. Second, we will study smooth converse Lyapunov theorems for degree-m estimates. In particular, under additional annular regularity assumptions on the nonsmooth value function, we expect to recover smooth Lyapunov certificates that preserve the degree-m dissipation structure. Third, we plan to connect the degree framework with computational methods for polynomial systems, including homogeneous approximations, rational Lyapunov functions, and sum-of-squares searches. Together, these extensions would turn the degree framework from a rate classification theory into a practical tool for identifying and certifying the sharp asymptotic decay order of nonlinear systems.
