# Geometric Hybrid Dynamical Systems: Part I – Modeling and Stability 

Piyush P. Jirwankar, Daniel E. Ochoa, and Ricardo G. Sanfelice 

Abstract — We present a framework for the modeling and analysis of geometric hybrid dynamical systems as hybrid inclusions on C1-manifolds. Using tools from non-smooth and set-valued analysis on manifolds, we derive coordinate-independent sufficient conditions for the exis-tence of nontrivial solutions to this type of systems. We present geometric notions of uniform stability and attrac-tivity of compact sets, and establish their equivalence to metric-based stability notions when the manifold is en-dowed with a Riemannian structure. We also introduce nonsmooth Lyapunov functions and a hybrid Lyapunov theorem providing sufficient conditions for uniform global asymptotic stability of compact sets. Finally, by charac-terizing ω-limit sets of precompact solutions, we derive a hybrid invariance principle for geometric hybrid dynamical systems. The results are demonstrated through several running examples. 

Index Terms — Hybrid dynamical systems, Lyapunov sta-bility, Invariance principle, Intrinsic manifolds. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

We study a class of hybrid dynamical systems that evolve on intrinsic manifolds; namely, geometric hybrid dynamical sys-tems . Several modeling frameworks exist for hybrid systems that evolve on the Euclidean space [1]–[3]. Among these, the hybrid inclusions framework [1] provides a mathematically well-developed setting that accommodates differential and difference inclusions with state constraints. However, these frameworks do not readily transfer to numerous mechanical and aerospace applications where states evolve on nonlinear manifolds, thus motivating the search for a geometric frame-work for hybrid dynamical systems on manifolds. 

A. On Embeddings and Metrizations 

A central issue in geometric models of hybrid systems is how the state-space manifold is treated. Although every smooth manifold can be embedded in a higher-dimensional Euclidean space by the Whitney embedding theorem [4, Thm. 6.15], its intrinsic properties do not depend on any particular embedding. Many frameworks [5], [6] assume an embedding in Rn to use Euclidean analytical and compu-tational tools. However, such an embedding adds structure that may be unavailable, noncanonical, or inconvenient. This issue is particularly relevant for robotic systems with quotient configuration spaces [7] or symplectic structure [8], where

For example, Nesterov-type acceleration on Riemannian manifolds depends critically on manifold curvature [9], which may be obscured by a non-isometric embedding. An intrinsic approach may also require additional metric structure for stability analysis. A common metric choice is a Riemannian metric, which provides a finite geodesic distance on connected manifolds [10, Prop. 16.14]. Hybrid state spaces, however, are often disconnected, with separate components representing different discrete modes [11]. Since geodesics do not connect these components, other distance functions must be introduced [4, Cor. 13.30]. Such distances may be noncanonical, causing the resulting Lyapunov analysis to depend on choices beyond the system’s intrinsic geometry. 

B. Existing Models of Hybrid Systems on Manifolds 

The foregoing discussion underscores the need for a purely topological and geometric framework for modeling hybrid dy-namical systems on intrinsic manifolds. The existing literature in this direction remains relatively limited. Early work in [12] models hybrid automata on submanifolds embedded in Eu-clidean space, where discrete “jumps” are represented through 

hybrifolds that transform hybrid dynamics into nonsmooth dynamics via certain quotient constructions. More recently, a fiber bundle–based formulation for hybrid automata with control inputs has been proposed in [13]. A more general modeling approach, namely “topological hybrid systems”, is introduced in [14]. While these contributions provide novel and insightful modeling paradigms, their dynamics are typically expressed through differential equations or semiflow relations, and are not naturally suited to capturing nonuniqueness of solutions or accommodating perturbations [15]. The hybrid inclusions framework addresses these issues systematically in the Eu-clidean setting, where nonuniqueness is incorporated through set-valued inclusion models [1]. An extension of this frame-work for hybrid systems on connected Riemannian manifolds is proposed in [16] to analyze multistable inclusions. However, this framework does not model hybrid automata, which is a large class of systems that exhibit disconnected underlying manifolds. Thus, to the best knowledge of the authors, no read-ily available framework combines the set-valued generality of hybrid inclusions with an intrinsic treatment of manifolds that admits disconnected state spaces. arXiv:2609.17687v2 [math.DS] 17 Sep 2026 2

C. Contributions 

Motivated by the limitations of embedding-based and Riemannian methods, this paper develops a coordinate-independent framework for hybrid systems on C1-manifolds. We call these systems geometric hybrid dynamical systems .The main contributions are as follows. 1) We provide sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions using regularity properties of set-valued maps between topological spaces (Proposition 4.2). 2) We introduce topological notions of uniform global sta-bility and uniform attractivity for compact sets (Defini-tions 5.1 and 5.4). These notions use neighborhoods rather than a chosen distance. For a class of Riemannian mani-folds, we show that the proposed stability notion is equiv-alent to a metric-based characterization (Proposition B.5). 3) We develop nonsmooth analysis tools on C1-manifolds, including tangent cones, locally Lipschitz functions, and present a Rademacher’s Theorem in manifolds as well as generalized directional derivatives (Definitions 4.1, 5.7,-5.9). We use these tools with a nonsmooth Lyapunov function to prove a hybrid Lyapunov theorem for uniform global asymptotic stability of compact sets (Theorem 5.15). 4) We provide conditions ensuring a closure property of the solution set (Theorem 4.6), characterize omega-limit sets (Proposition 6.3), and derive a hybrid invariance principle on C1-manifolds (Theorem 6.5). 5) We illustrate the framework through two running examples. The first is hybrid attitude stabilization on S3 (Exam-ples 3.5, 4.3, and 5.16). The second is stabilization of billiard ball on the M¨ obius band (Examples 3.6, 4.4, and 6.6), which is a second-order system with impacts. We highlight that our presentation does not require choosing a metric or an embedding. However, it allows using one when convenient, as demonstrated through the examples. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first work to collect and extend the above results to hybrid dynamical systems evolving on abstract manifolds in a fully metric-free, embedding-free, and coordinate-independent setting. 

Relation with previous work: Preliminary results from this work were presented in the conference papers [17], [18], which omitted intermediate steps, detailed proofs, and additional results. We improve over those versions in the following ways: (i) we introduce Theorem 4.6, which generalizes [18, Thm. 2] by allowing a broader class of solution sequences; (ii) we generalize [17, Prop. 3] to the Riemannian manifold setting in Propositions B.5, showing that metric-based and topological notions of uniform global stability coincide. We also obtain several auxiliary results, including (iii) the co-ordinate independence of the geometric definition of tangent cones (Lemma B.1); and (iv) a revised definition of locally Lipschitz functions between C1-manifolds from [17, Def. 5] (Definition 5.7), and a manifold version of Rademacher’s theorem (Proposition B.2). 

Organization: Section II introduces notation and the nec-essary preliminaries from differential geometry and set-valued analysis on manifolds. Section III introduces geometric hybrid systems and related notions. Existence of solution and prop-erties of solution sets are studied in Section IV. Section V presents topological notions of (uniform) asymptotic stability and a hybrid Lyapunov theorem. Section VI presents a hybrid invariance principle. Some proofs and auxiliary results are presented in the Appendix.

To obtain a hybrid Lyapunov theorem that certifies uniform global asymptotic stability of a compact set for H, we first define several topological notions of uniform stability, attrac-tivity, and asymptotic stability. Unlike their Euclidean coun-terparts [1, Def. 3.6], the proposed notions are independent of any metric on M, and rely solely on the topology of M.

A. Stability and Attractivity Notions on C1-Manifolds 

We begin by proposing notions of (uniform) stability, attrac-tivity, and asymptotic stability of a compact set A ⊂ M for 

H. Recall that as M is a C1-manifold, we may not know the Riemannian metric on it. Hence, the distance between points on this manifold may not be defined. The following definitions are formulated considering this fact. 

Definition 5.1 (Uniform Global Stability): Given a geo-metric hybrid system H = (C, F, D, G, M), a nonempty, compact set A ⊂ M is said to be 1) uniformly Lyapunov stable (ULS) for H if, for each neighborhood U of A, there exists a neighborhood W

of A such that, for each ϕ ∈ S H(W ), rge ϕ ⊂ U ;2) uniformly Lagrange stable (ULaS) for H if, for each compact neighborhood W of A, there exists a compact neighborhood U of A such that, for each ϕ ∈ S H(W ),

rge ϕ ⊂ U ;3) uniformly globally stable (UGS) for H if it is Lyapunov stable and uniformly Lagrange stable for H.

Remark 5.2: In metric spaces, uniform global stability can equivalently be introduced through a direct K∞ bound on solutions [1, Def. 3.6], or through growth conditions on the dependence of δ on ε in an ε-δ definition of Lyapunov stability formulated via metric inflations of A [32, Prop. 2]. Instead, in Definition 5.1, we follow the decomposition into uniform Lyapunov and uniform Lagrange stability as in [32, Thm. 1] for nonlinear systems. Replacing metric inflations by suitable neighborhoods recasts this decomposition as a sep-aration between topological and bornological (boundedness) content [33] and admits a metric-independent realization. ♦

To relate Definition 5.1 to an easily verifiable notion for systems on Riemannian manifolds, we show in Proposition B.5 that, when A is compact and M is a complete Riemannian manifold, the above definition is equivalent to the existence of a class-K∞ function that upper bounds, at each hybrid time, the Riemannian distance from each solution to the A. Finally, we present notions of attractivity and asymptotic stability. 

Definition 5.3 (Pre-attractivity): A nonempty, closed set 

A ⊂ M is pre-attractive (pA) for H if there exists an open neighborhood U ⊂ M of A such that 1) for each ϕ ∈ S H(U ) with length(dom ϕ) < ∞, rge ϕ ⊂M is precompact; 2) for each complete ϕ ∈ S H(U ) and each open neigh-borhood W ⊂ M of A, there exists N ≥ 0 such that 

(t, j ) ∈ dom ϕ and t + j ≥ N implies that ϕ(t, j ) ∈ W .The uniform counterpart of the above definition is provided below when A is compact. 

Definition 5.4 (Uniform global pre-attractivity): A non-empty, compact set A is uniformly globally pre-attractive (UGpA) for H if, for each compact neighborhoods U, W ⊂ M 

of A, there exists N ≥ 0 such that for each ϕ ∈ S H(U ),

(t, j ) ∈ dom ϕ and t + j ≥ N implies ϕ(t, j ) ∈ W .

Definition 5.5 (Pre-asymptotic stability): A nonempty, compact set A ⊂ M is pre-asymptotically stable (pAS) for 

H if it is ULS and pA for H.

Definition 5.6 (Uniform global pre-asymptotic stability): 

A nonempty, compact set A ⊂ M is uniformly globally 10 

pre-asymptotically stable (UGpAS) for H if it is UGS and UGpA for H.In the definitions above, the prefix “pre-” enables maximal solutions to not be complete. If each maximal solution to H

is complete, then the prefix is dropped. 

B. Hybrid Lyapunov Theorem on C1-Manifolds

Inspired by the existing results in the Euclidean case [25, Thm. 3.19], we admit Lyapunov function candidates that are merely locally Lipschitz rather than continuously differentiable. This relaxation calls for additional nonsmooth analysis tools on manifolds, which we develop before stating the stability certificates. We begin by introducing a suitable definition of locally Lipschitz functions. This notion extends the one in [28] and specializes [34, Def. 3], and differs from the one in [17].

VII. C ONCLUSION 

We have proposed a framework for the formulation and analysis of hybrid dynamical systems whose state evolves on a C1-manifold, which we call geometric hybrid dynamical systems. The framework separates the minimal geometric structure required to analyze such systems from common as-sumptions that may carry information not naturally associated with the dynamics, and in particular it is independent of structure that is extrinsic to the underlying manifold, such as a Riemannian metric or an embedding into Euclidean space. Future work includes: (i) formulating robustness properties of geometric hybrid systems with respect to structural per-turbations, along with minimal assumptions on their data, in the spirit of [1, Ch. 6]; and (ii) extending the framework to geometric hybrid dynamical systems with inputs, which would enable control-synthesis methods and input-to-state stability certificates with respect to measurable disturbances.
