# Fixed-Time ISS for Interconnected Discrete-Time Systems, Part I: Extinction-Based Lyapunov Functions 

Sumadhu Rubaiyat and Jorge I. Poveda. 

Abstract — We study fixed-time input-to-state stability (FxT-ISS) for discrete-time systems subject to exogenous inputs. We introduce Lyapunov-based sufficient conditions for FxT-ISS based on a class of dissipation functions with a fixed-time extinction property, which encompasses commonly used two-power constructions while allowing substantially more general decay mechanisms. Building on this framework, we establish a small-gain theorem for interconnected FxT-ISS systems in the presence of disturbances. The results are illustrated through several examples, including perturbed optimization algorithms and a discrete-time feedback-optimization architecture. 

I. INTRODUCTION Traditionally, discrete-time control, optimization, and decision-making algorithms have been analyzed using tools that certify asymptotic stability [1], [2]. This property com-bines stability and asymptotic attractivity: the former pre-vents the iterates from undergoing arbitrarily large excur-sions away from their nominal operating point, while the latter guarantees convergence to that point as time tends to infinity. However, increasing computational and performance requirements have motivated the development of algorithms for which convergence is not merely asymptotic, but occurs exactly within a uniformly bounded time. This behavior is captured by the notion of fixed-time (FxT) stability. In the continuous-time setting, since its discovery in [3], fixed-time stability has received significant attention and has led to new algorithmic architectures for control [4], [5], estimation [6], learning [7], and optimization [8]. In contrast, fixed-time stability remains considerably less developed in the discrete-time setting. A formalization of this property, together with Lyapunov-based certificates for a class of discrete-time systems, was introduced in [9], with related results subsequently extended to certain stochastic systems [10], [11]. More recently, the observations in [12] showed that fixed-time stable discrete-time dynamics can also arise from suitable discretizations of continuous-time controllers. However, despite these developments, several fundamental questions remain open. Existing results have largely focused on particular classes of discrete-time dynamics built around the two-power structures commonly used in continuous-time fixed-time stability [9], [11], and more general Lyapunov characterizations remain comparatively unexplored. More importantly, the effect of exogenous inputs and disturbances has received little attention, and existing results have focused on constructive tools for sampled-data controllers in time-varying linear systems with disturbance bounds depending

Likewise, systematic conditions guaranteeing preservation of fixed-time stability under in-terconnection, analogous to fixed-time small-gain results in continuous time [14], [15], or finite-time results in discrete-time [16] are still largely unavailable. In this paper, we develop a framework for the analysis of fixed-time stability in discrete-time systems subject to exogenous inputs, with particular emphasis on interconnected systems. First, we introduce the notion of fixed-time input-to-state stability (FxT-ISS) for discrete-time systems with re-spect to sets using (generalized) KL functions, and establish Lyapunov-based sufficient conditions to certify this property. A key technical ingredient is a new class of Lyapunov dissipation functions characterized by a fixed-time extinction property : rather than imposing a particular algebraic form on the Lyapunov decrease, this property requires the associated scalar comparison dynamics to vanish after a uniformly bounded number of iterations. This viewpoint provides a unifying framework that recovers the two-power and expo-nential decay mechanisms studied in [9] and [11], while substantially enlarging the class of admissible dissipation functions and providing a more flexible mechanism for certifying fixed-time convergence in discrete time, enabling the uniform incorporation of exogenous disturbances in the analysis via ISS tools. Building on this characterization, and inspired by recent results in continuous-time [14], [17], we then develop a fixed-time small-gain-type theorem 

for interconnected discrete-time systems whose individual components satisfy suitable FxT-ISS properties. The result provides conditions under which the fixed-time behavior of the individual subsystems is preserved under feedback interconnection, while explicitly accounting for exogenous disturbances. Together, these results provide one particu-lar extension of the Lyapunov and small-gain machinery commonly available for asymptotically stable discrete-time systems to the fixed-time regime. For alternative Lyapunov-based characterizations suitable for FxT-ISS systems that do not admit the Lyapunov dissipation functions studied in this paper, we refer the reader to the companion paper [18]. VI. CONCLUSIONS This paper developed a framework for the study of fixed-time input-to-state stability in discrete-time systems. Using a class of dissipation functions characterized by a fixed-time extinction property, we established Lyapunov-based suf-ficient conditions for FxT-ISS that extend beyond commonly used two-power constructions. Building on these results, we developed a small-gain theorem for interconnected FxT-ISS systems, providing conditions under which fixed-time stability is preserved in the presence of interconnections.
