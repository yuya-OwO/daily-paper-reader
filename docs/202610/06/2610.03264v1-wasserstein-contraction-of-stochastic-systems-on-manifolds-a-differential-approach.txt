WASSERSTEIN CONTRACTION OF STOCHASTIC SYSTEMS ON
MANIFOLDS: A DIFFERENTIAL APPROACH
DONGJUN WU
Abstract. Contraction analysis of dynamical systems turns a global convergence problem into a
local one. If the variational system is stable in a uniform sense, then all trajectories of the original
system converge toward each other. This paper develops a stochastic analogue for continuous-time
stochastic systems, where convergence is measured by Wasserstein distance between probability laws.
The key step is to employ mean-square differentiation to construct stochastic variational dynamics.
Its uniform L2 stability can then be integrated along curves of initial conditions, yielding Wasserstein
contraction of the associated probability laws. This yields criteria formulated in terms of differential
Lyapunov functions, and on manifolds, it leads to intrinsic Riemannian formulations. A distinctive
feature of the stochastic setting is that the coupling of the noises becomes a design freedom. To
exploit this freedom, we introduce an orthogonal differential coupling that extends the framework
beyond synchronous coupling. The resulting criterion explicitly exposes how the curvature of the state
space enters the contraction mechanism and, in the orthonormal-frame case, recovers the classical
Ricci-curvature structure.
Finally, we apply the framework to stochastic control on a compact
manifold. By introducing stochastic control input, we overcome a deterministic obstruction to global
stabilization. That is, a system that cannot be globally stabilized through smooth time-invariant
deterministic feedback is rendered globally contracting in W2, and its steady state distribution can
be made arbitrarily concentrated around a prescribed target equilibrium.
1. Introduction. Contraction analysis provides a differential approach to increm-
ental stability. Instead of studying convergence to a prescribed equilibrium or reference
trajectory, it asks whether any two solutions of a dynamical system converge toward
one another. For a deterministic system
(1.1)
˙x = f(t, x),
such convergence is implied by uniform (asymptotic or exponential) stability of the
following variational dynamics
˙δx = ∂f
∂x(t, x)δx,
which characterizes the local or infinitesimal behavior of the system (1.1). Thus
the variational system turns a global convergence question to a local one.
This
principle dates back to the work of Demidovich [12] and was developed systematically
by Lohmiller and Slotine [25, 26].
It has since led to abundant applications to
system analysis [11, 15, 35, 39], and synthesis [10, 27, 33, 34, 37, 45]. A fundamental
development is the replacement of a fixed Euclidean metric by a state-dependent
Riemannian, or more generally Finsler metric [13, 44].
Most of the existing literature on contraction analysis is concerned with determinist-
ic systems. In recent years, there has been a growing interest to extend the theories
to stochastic systems. Such an extension is relevant for control systems subject to
process noise and state-dependent stochastic disturbances, for which contraction of
individual sample paths is generally not expected. Different contraction metrics of
stochastic systems have thus been considered in the literature, such as mean-square
contraction [31, 30, 19], moment contraction [2], and Wasserstein contraction of
probability measures [6]. These results were typically obtained through independent-
noise comparisons and therefore contain a noise-dependent residual.
More recently, stochastic contraction has been investigated under common stochas-
tic noise. For discrete-time systems, [17, 18] developed moment-contraction results.

In continuous time, [16] established Wp incremental input-to-state stability for state-

In this paper, we study Wasserstein contraction of the probability laws of continuou-
s-time stochastic systems. Let µt and νt denote the laws at time t of the same stochastic
system with initial laws µ0 and ν0, respectively. We seek estimates of the form
(1.2)

where Wp is the p-Wasserstein distance and K ≥1, λ > 0 are constants.
Wp contraction can be characterized in terms of the diffusion semigroup associated
with the underlying stochastic system and is therefore closely linked to properties of
the semigroup. From this perspective, methods based-on gradient estimates, functional
inequalities, geometric properties of the underlying state space, and explicit coupling
constructions have been extensively developed; see, e.g., [5, 8, 22, 42, 43, 29]. These
methods have led to powerful contraction results, especially for diffusions on manifolds,
but often rely on the structures of the state space or the systems.
Within the contraction-theoretic literature, Wasserstein contraction have been
established in [6, 16]. The estimate in [6] contains a noise-dependent residual arising
from an independent-noise comparison, whereas the residual-free Wasserstein result in
[16] assumes state-independent diffusion and a drift contracting in a fixed norm. In the
latter setting, the diffusion terms cancel under synchronous coupling, so contraction
follows from the contraction property of the drift. For state-dependent diffusion, this
cancellation generally no longer holds, and derivatives of the diffusion fields enter the
stochastic variational dynamics.
To address these limitations, we develop contraction conditions using mean-square
variational systems and an L2 calculus of stochastic processes. In Euclidean space, the
trajectory of a stochastic system at each fixed time is viewed as an L2-valued function of
its initial condition. Its mean-square derivative satisfies a stochastic variational system
involving the derivatives of both the drift and the diffusion fields. The L2 calculus
allows differential contraction estimates for this system to be integrated along curves of
initial conditions, yielding estimates between coupled solutions. An optimal coupling
of the initial laws then turns these estimates into Wasserstein contraction. This
construction follows the local-to-global principle of deterministic contraction analysis.
That is, rather than establishing semigroup estimates directly, we deduce contraction
of probability laws from contraction of the stochastic variational dynamics. The
differential conditions accommodate state-dependent diffusion and general differential
Lyapunov functions, including time- and state-dependent contraction metrics. Based on
the results in Euclidean space, we extend the construction intrinsically to Riemannian
manifolds and incorporate orthogonal differential coupling, so that both the geometry
of the state space and the choice of coupling enter the contraction criterion.

The contributions of the paper can be summarized as follows.
1. We develop a differential approach for Wasserstein contraction and establish
condition (1.5). The main technique is to introduce a mean-square variation
of the stochastic system, mimicking the conventional deterministic variational
systems. The Euclidean result allows general Finsler-Lyapunov function and,
in particular, time- and state-dependent contraction metrics.
2. We formulate the construction intrinsically on Riemannian manifolds using
stochastic prolongation to the tangent bundle. This gives the Lie-derivative
criterion (1.7), providing a direct stochastic analogue of deterministic Rieman-
nian contraction.
3. We introduce orthogonal differential coupling as an additional degree of
freedom and derive the curvature-dependent condition (1.9). The construction
explains the conservatism of synchronous coupling, recovers the Ricci-curvature
criterion in the orthonormal-frame case, and yields sharp contraction certifica-
tes for representative stochastic systems on manifolds such as the sphere,
SO(n) and the space of positive definite matrices.
4. We illustrate the control implications through noise-assisted stabilization on
a compact manifold, where a smooth deterministic feedback is prevented
from global stabilizatizing an equilibrium. By adding a suitably shaped state-
dependent diffusion noise, the resulting closed-loop system becomes globally
exponentially contracting in W2 and possesses a unique invariant distribution
4


that can be made arbitrarily concentrated around the desired target while
retaining a strictly positive contraction rate.
The presentation follows a bottom-up route. We first develop the Euclidean theory,
where the mean-square variational arguments are most transparent. We then employ
the construction to Riemannian manifolds and obtain the intrinsic synchronous criterion
(1.7). The limitations of synchronous coupling motivate the subsequent differential-
coupling theory, first through its infinitesimal interpretation and then in its intrinsic
manifold form. Finally, the geometric results are applied to noise-assisted stabilization
on the 2-sphere.

7. Conclusion. This paper developed a contraction-analysis approach to Wassers-
tein contraction of stochastic systems. The main idea is to retain the differential
viewpoint underlying deterministic contraction theory. In Euclidean space, this yields
criteria based on general Finsler-Lyapunov functions, leading to both W1 and W2
contraction results.
A feature that distinguishes the stochastic problem from its deterministic counterpa-
rt is the freedom to couple the driving noises. Synchronous coupling provides a natural
starting point, but it can be conservative. The orthogonal differential coupling used
in this paper incorporates this freedom directly into the variational dynamics. On
manifolds, the resulting criterion recovers the familiar curvature-based contraction
condition in the orthonormal-frame case. The examples on the sphere and on Lie
groups illustrate that this additional coupling freedom can reveal contraction that is
hidden by synchronous coupling.
