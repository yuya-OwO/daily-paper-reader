# Nesterov-Accelerated Concurrent Learning for Lyapunov-Based Deep Neural Networks 

Hossein Papi 1, Omkar Sudhir Patil 1

Abstract —Concurrent learning (CL) has recently been ex-tended to Lyapunov-based deep neural networks (DNNs) to obtain parameter convergence under finite rather than persistent excitation. However, the existing first-order law converges slowly, especially for high-dimensional systems and overparameterized networks. To address this problem, this paper develops aNesterov-accelerated concurrent learning (NACL) adaptation law for uncertain second-order control-affine systems. The developed law drives momentum dynamics with recorded state-input data, so that all hidden layers of the DNN are updated online under a verifiable finite excitation condition. A dynamic state-derivative observer is designed to reconstruct the recorded input without acceleration measurements. The momentum variable introduces a cross term coupling the momentum lag error to the state-dependent DNN Jacobian and the tracking error, and a state-dependent normalization is introduced into the momentum filter to dominate it. A Lyapunov-based analysis establishes exponential convergence of the tracking, momentum, observer, and weight estimation errors to a bounded residual ball. Simulations on a six-degree-of-freedom unmanned underwater vehicle demonstrate a 

24 .5% and 62 .9% reduction in the root-mean-square function approximation error relative to first-order CL and to a higher-order tuner without CL, respectively. 

Index Terms —Adaptive control, deep neural networks, higher order tuner, concurrent learning, Lyapunov methods, nonlinear control systems. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# DEEP neural networks (DNNs) are universal function approximators [1], [2] and have long been used to compensate for unstructured uncertainty in nonlinear systems [3]–[7]. Conventional DNN-based controllers are trained of-fline on a fixed dataset and held constant during operation, so no guarantee relates the learned model to the dynamics encountered at run time. Lyapunov-based DNNs (Lb-DNNs) remove the offline stage by deriving the adaptation law for every layer from a stability analysis [8]–[13], which yields real-time weight adaptation with a guarantee on the tracking error. However, a guarantee on the tracking error is not a guarantee on learning the model. Since the update laws of [8], [10] are driven by the instantaneous tracking error alone, the weight estimates need not converge, and the identified DNN cannot be relied upon for prediction, fault detection, or operation without continuous feedback [14]. Parameter convergence classically requires persistence of excitation (PE) [15]–[18]. For a DNN the condition falls on the network Jacobian, which depends on the weight estimates themselves, so the requirement is

Data-driven adaptation replaces PE with initial, interval, or finite excitation (FE) conditions imposed on recorded signals, through dynamic regressor extension and mixing [19]– [21], matrix regressors [22], and filter-based and integral constructions [23]–[26]. Concurrent learning (CL) [27]–[29], integral CL [30], and composite learning [31], [32] obtain parameter convergence by driving the update with a prediction error formed from a history stack, so that the excitation condition becomes verifiable online. Extending CL to a DNN is challenging because of the nonlinear-in-parameter (NIP) dependence on the inner-layer weights: the prediction error is not affine in the estimates, and the Jacobian that takes its place evolves with them. Lb-DNN controllers have been augmented with CL for control-affine and Euler-Lagrange plants [33]– [35], and the all-layer case was addressed in [36] by recon-structing the history stack from the current Jacobian. However, the Jacobian history stack of a deep network is severely ill-conditioned, since inner-layer directions are excited only through products of outer-layer weights, so the first-order law of [36] learns at the rate of its weakest recorded direction. The first-order structure of the update law itself has been revisited. Higher-order tuners (HOTs), motivated by the continuous-time limit of Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method [37]–[39], add an auxiliary momentum state and a state-dependent normalization, and improve the convergence rate for constant and time-varying regressors [40]–[45]. Mo-mentum and concurrent learning were combined for linear-in-the-parameters Euler-Lagrange systems in [46], where an integral CL term drives an accelerated gradient update under finite excitation. The accelerated structure reaches the DNN setting in [47], where all weights adapt in real time and asymptotic tracking is established, but without recorded data. Combining momentum with concurrent learning for a DNN is not a matter of replacing the update law of [36]. A higher-order law splits the weight estimation error into two coupled states, the momentum variable and the lag of the estimates behind it, and a single CL gain multiplies both. Consequently, the recorded-data term damps one state while exciting the other and is no longer purely stabilizing: a richer history stack drives the estimates toward their ideal values and drives the lag harder at the same time. Moreover, the momentum variable introduces a cross term among the lag, the state-dependent DNN Jacobian, and the tracking error that has no first-order counterpart. Higher-order tuners for linear-in-the-parameters uncertainty dominate the analogous term with a normalization built from a known regressor, whereas the DNN Jacobian arXiv:2610.04200v1 [eess.SY] 3 Oct 2026 is evaluated at the current estimates, so the normalization is estimate dependent and its boundedness must be established rather than assumed. This paper develops a Nesterov-accelerated concurrent learning (NACL) adaptation law for Lyapunov-based DNNs, together with the stability analysis that resolves the aforemen-tioned obstructions. Relative to [46], which combined momen-tum and CL for known regressors, the history stack here acts through the current-weight Jacobian, and the Taylor remainder and the normalization must be designed rather than assumed. The central element is a dual state-dependent normalization matched to the two obstructions above. Projection of the weight estimates and the momentum variable ensures that the normalizers, the Jacobian, and the Taylor remainder remain uniformly bounded, and a dynamic state-derivative observer reconstructs the recorded control input without acceleration measurements. A Lyapunov-based analysis shows that the tracking, momentum, weight estimation, and observer errors converge exponentially to an ultimate bound under a finite excitation condition on the recorded Jacobian data, rather than under persistent excitation of the trajectory. Simulations on a six-degree-of-freedom unmanned underwater vehicle show that momentum without recorded data performs worse than first-order CL, whereas the developed law reduces the root-mean-square function approximation error by 62 .9% relative to the momentum tuner and by 24 .5% relative to first-order CL.

A. Setup 

The developed method is evaluated on the six-degree-of-freedom unmanned underwater vehicle of [14], with the mass, inertia and hydrodynamic damping of [50]. The body-fixed inertia is 

Mb = diag(215 , 265 , 265 , 40 , 80 , 80) , the linear and quadratic damping coefficients are diag(70 , 100 , 200 , 30 , 50 , 50) 

and diag(100 , 200 , 50 , 50 , 100 , 100) , and the vehicle is neutrally buoyant. The state x ∈ R6 collects the earth-frame position and orientation, so X ∈ R12 and n = 6 ,and the input u in (1) is the commanded earth-frame acceleration, from which the applied wrench follows through the known inertia matrix. The desired trajectory is the helix 

xd(t) = [2 cos(0 .5t), 2 sin(0 .5t), 0.1t, 0, 0, −0.125 t]⊤,initialized at x(t0) = [−0.5, −0.5, −0.5, 0, 0, 0] ⊤ and 

˙x(t0) = 0 , and each run is integrated for 30 s at a fixed step of 10 ms; the linearly growing depth and yaw are understood to be continued by a bounded profile after the simulated interval, so that the bounds on xd hold. The DNN has k = 4 hidden layers of 12 neurons with 

tanh activations and a bias appended to every layer, so nθ =702 . All methods use identical initial weights, the same plant, and the same observer. The control and observer gains are 

α1 = 5 , k1 = 20 , α2 = 50 and k∆ = 20 . The gating time 

t∆ = 5 s was selected conservatively; the observer error ˜∆

falls below 0.03 within 2 s in every run. The history stack holds N = 150 samples. Every second sample after t∆ is retained until the stack is full; afterwards a candidate sample is examined every 50 ms and replaces the oldest retained sample only if the rank and minimum eigenvalue of ΦJ , evaluated at the current weights, do not decrease. The retained states 

Xi are never otherwise altered, and their Jacobians are re-evaluated at ˆθ(t) at every step. The CL gains are γ1 = 20 and 

γ2 = 10 −3, the gain law (20) uses κ = 0 .1 and ν = 0 .05 with 

Γ( t0) = 0 .5I confined to the band [10 −4, 10] , the higher-order gain is β = 0 .5, and μ1 and μ2 are set to 1.05 times the lower bounds in (35). The projection bound is ¯θ = 100 .Three methods are compared. The Lb-CL-DNN of [36] is a first-order CL law using the same history stack, replacement rule, and observer, with its gain law run at ν = 1 and a forgetting factor of 0.1. The A-DNN of [47] is a higher-order tuner without CL, run with its own control law and the gains γν = 0.5, γθ = 4 and βs = 0.05 reported therein. The Lb-DNN baseline is the first-order gradient law of [8] with constant gain 0.5I. Performance is quantified over

The off-trajectory errors of the two CL methods are within 2% of each other. Hence, the improvement of the developed law over [36] lies in the accuracy of learning along the recorded data rather than in generalization beyond it; since the two laws also differ in the covariance weighting ν, the comparison does not attribute the improvement to the momentum channel alone. VI. C ONCLUSION 

A Nesterov-accelerated concurrent learning (NACL) adapta-tion law was developed for a Lyapunov-based DNN controller that updates all layers online, in which the momentum variable is driven by a finitely exciting history stack and the higher-order cross term is dominated by a state-dependent normal-ization.
