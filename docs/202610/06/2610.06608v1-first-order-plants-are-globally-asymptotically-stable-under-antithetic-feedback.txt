# First-order plants are globally asymptotically stable under antithetic feedback 

Wouter Jongeneel 

Abstract — We show that first-order linear plants are globally asymptotically stable under antithetic feedback. This is shown by constructing an explicit smooth, convex Lyapunov function and by appealing to a Krasovskii-LaSalle invariance principle. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Antithetic feedback, as pioneered by Briat, Gupta and Khammash [1], is the biological manifestation of integral control. In this note we study the most basic version, that is, with just a single node representing a first-order linear plant. Despite the corresponding ODE being merely a 3-dimensional bilinear system, global stability remains elusive, 

e.g. , see [2] for further comments. Below, we provide a Lya-punov function and conclude on global asymptotic stability, affirmatively. II. A NTITHETIC FEEDBACK

Although Theorem II.1 holds for any choice of parameters, one cannot extend the result to any interaction that matches the motif from Figure 1 (I). For instance, if we replace the degradation term −γx by the saturated degradation term 

−γ1x/ (γ2 + x), the equilibrium can become unstable and one will observe oscillations. This also means that structural results like [10] do not apply and a different form of a “generic” robustness analysis needs to be found. See [11] for a recent and more extensive discussion in this direction. III. D ISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 

The deceptively simple dynamical system (1) seems to resist a simple global stability analysis; as shown in this note, a more involved analysis is possible. Towards the global analysis of more interesting controllers, a more principled analysis would be welcome. Some directions are explored in [4], [7]. REFERENCES [1] C. Briat, A. Gupta, and M.
