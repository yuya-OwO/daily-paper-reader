WASSERSTEIN CONTRACTION OF STOCHASTIC SYSTEMS ON MANIFOLDS: A DIFFERENTIAL APPROACH 

DONGJUN WU 

Abstract. Contraction analysis of dynamical systems turns a global convergence problem into a local one. If the variational system is stable in a uniform sense, then all trajectories of the original system converge toward each other. This paper develops a stochastic analogue for continuous-time stochastic systems, where convergence is measured by Wasserstein distance between probability laws. The key step is to employ mean-square differentiation to construct stochastic variational dynamics. Its uniform L2 stability can then be integrated along curves of initial conditions, yielding Wasserstein contraction of the associated probability laws. This yields criteria formulated in terms of differential Lyapunov functions, and on manifolds, it leads to intrinsic Riemannian formulations. A distinctive feature of the stochastic setting is that the coupling of the noises becomes a design freedom. To exploit this freedom, we introduce an orthogonal differential coupling that extends the framework beyond synchronous coupling. The resulting criterion explicitly exposes how the curvature of the state space enters the contraction mechanism and, in the orthonormal-frame case, recovers the classical Ricci-curvature structure. Finally, we apply the framework to stochastic control on a compact manifold. By introducing stochastic control input, we overcome a deterministic obstruction to global stabilization. That is, a system that cannot be globally stabilized through smooth time-invariant deterministic feedback is rendered globally contracting in W2, and its steady state distribution can be made arbitrarily concentrated around a prescribed target equilibrium. 

1. Introduction. Contraction analysis provides a differential approach to increm-ental stability. Instead of studying convergence to a prescribed equilibrium or reference trajectory, it asks whether any two solutions of a dynamical system converge toward one another. For a deterministic system (1.1) ˙x = f (t, x ),

such convergence is implied by uniform (asymptotic or exponential) stability of the following variational dynamics 

˙δx = ∂f ∂x (t, x )δx, 

which characterizes the local or infinitesimal behavior of the system (1.1). Thus the variational system turns a global convergence question to a local one. This principle dates back to the work of Demidovich [12] and was developed systematically by Lohmiller and Slotine [25, 26]. It has since led to abundant applications to system analysis [11, 15, 35, 39], and synthesis [10, 27, 33, 34, 37, 45]. A fundamental development is the replacement of a fixed Euclidean metric by a state-dependent Riemannian, or more generally Finsler metric [13, 44]. Most of the existing literature on contraction analysis is concerned with determinist-ic systems. In recent years, there has been a growing interest to extend the theories to stochastic systems. Such an extension is relevant for control systems subject to process noise and state-dependent stochastic disturbances, for which contraction of individual sample paths is generally not expected. Different contraction metrics of stochastic systems have thus been considered in the literature, such as mean-square contraction [31, 30, 19], moment contraction [2], and Wasserstein contraction of probability measures [6]. These results were typically obtained through independent-noise comparisons and therefore contain a noise-dependent residual. More recently, stochastic contraction has been investigated under common stochas-tic noise. For discrete-time systems, [17, 18] developed moment-contraction results. 

1arXiv:2610.03264v1 [math.OC] 2 Oct 2026 In continuous time, [16] established Wp incremental input-to-state stability for state-independent, time-varying diffusion and a drift contracting in a fixed norm. Almost-sure contraction has been studied in [28, 19], with [19] also addressing Lp stochastic contraction. In this paper, we study Wasserstein contraction of the probability laws of continuou-s-time stochastic systems. Let μt and νt denote the laws at time t of the same stochastic system with initial laws μ0 and ν0, respectively. We seek estimates of the form (1.2) Wp(μt, ν t) ≤ Ke −λt Wp(μ0, ν 0), t ≥ 0,

where Wp is the p-Wasserstein distance and K ≥ 1, λ > 0 are constants. 

Wp contraction can be characterized in terms of the diffusion semigroup associated with the underlying stochastic system and is therefore closely linked to properties of the semigroup. From this perspective, methods based-on gradient estimates, functional inequalities, geometric properties of the underlying state space, and explicit coupling constructions have been extensively developed; see, e.g., [5, 8, 22, 42, 43, 29]. These methods have led to powerful contraction results, especially for diffusions on manifolds, but often rely on the structures of the state space or the systems. Within the contraction-theoretic literature, Wasserstein contraction have been established in [6, 16]. The estimate in [6] contains a noise-dependent residual arising from an independent-noise comparison, whereas the residual-free Wasserstein result in [16] assumes state-independent diffusion and a drift contracting in a fixed norm. In the latter setting, the diffusion terms cancel under synchronous coupling, so contraction follows from the contraction property of the drift. For state-dependent diffusion, this cancellation generally no longer holds, and derivatives of the diffusion fields enter the stochastic variational dynamics. To address these limitations, we develop contraction conditions using mean-square variational systems and an L2 calculus of stochastic processes. In Euclidean space, the trajectory of a stochastic system at each fixed time is viewed as an L2-valued function of its initial condition. Its mean-square derivative satisfies a stochastic variational system involving the derivatives of both the drift and the diffusion fields. The L2 calculus allows differential contraction estimates for this system to be integrated along curves of initial conditions, yielding estimates between coupled solutions. An optimal coupling of the initial laws then turns these estimates into Wasserstein contraction. This construction follows the local-to-global principle of deterministic contraction analysis. That is, rather than establishing semigroup estimates directly, we deduce contraction of probability laws from contraction of the stochastic variational dynamics. The differential conditions accommodate state-dependent diffusion and general differential Lyapunov functions, including time- and state-dependent contraction metrics. Based on the results in Euclidean space, we extend the construction intrinsically to Riemannian manifolds and incorporate orthogonal differential coupling, so that both the geometry of the state space and the choice of coupling enter the contraction criterion. 3. Wasserstein Contraction in Euclidean Spaces . We first develop the differential contraction theory in Euclidean space. The construction follows the same local-to-global principle as deterministic contraction analysis: an infinitesimal displacement is propagated by a variational system, and its decay is integrated along a curve of initial conditions to obtain a finite-distance estimate. The essential difference is that regular differentiability is lost in the stochastic case. We therefore need a weaker notion of derivative called mean-square derivative . This notion is not new and well-known in stochastic systems, see for example, [3, Chapter 7], or [32, Chapter V]. Throughout this section, we consider the Itˆ o diffusion (1.3) introduced in Section 2.

7. Conclusion. This paper developed a contraction-analysis approach to Wassers-tein contraction of stochastic systems. The main idea is to retain the differential viewpoint underlying deterministic contraction theory. In Euclidean space, this yields criteria based on general Finsler-Lyapunov functions, leading to both W1 and W2

contraction results. A feature that distinguishes the stochastic problem from its deterministic counterpa-rt is the freedom to couple the driving noises. Synchronous coupling provides a natural starting point, but it can be conservative. The orthogonal differential coupling used in this paper incorporates this freedom directly into the variational dynamics. On manifolds, the resulting criterion recovers the familiar curvature-based contraction condition in the orthonormal-frame case. The examples on the sphere and on Lie groups illustrate that this additional coupling freedom can reveal contraction that is hidden by synchronous coupling.

By shaping the diffusion as a control input, noise can be used to modify the global behavior of the probability law. The results also leave several directions open. For example, how to use Wasserstein contraction results for observer design, in which case, non-synchronous noise has to be taken into consideration.
