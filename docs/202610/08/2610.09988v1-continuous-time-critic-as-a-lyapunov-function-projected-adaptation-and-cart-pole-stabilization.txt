# Continuous-Time Critic as a Lyapunov Function: Projected Adaptation and Cart-Pole Stabilization 

## Pavel Osinenko ∗

Abstract 

We study adaptive stabilization of known control-affine systems by treating the current parameterized critic itself as a Lyapunov candidate. Its total derivative contains a parameter-velocity term that can compensate unfavorable state evolution. A projection onto the intersection of a bounded admissible velocity set and one decay halfspace enforces parameter invariance and Lyapunov decrease simultaneously. We give an exact pointwise feasibility test, a conditional state-stability theorem, and a counterexample showing why a positive critic family containing a stabilizing member does not imply persistent feasibility. A convex endpoint constraint provides a distinct sampled implementation. A generic periodic polynomial-factor critic with a local Riccati anchor is evaluated on a force-limited cart-pole without mechanical-energy features, replay training, waypoints, or controller switching. The frozen controller passes stabilization and numerical decay checks on 600 sampled initial states across two draw sets; 400 matched continuous trajectories also pass. Independent reintegration audits and a conservative local feasibility certificate are distinguished from a global or machine-arithmetic guarantee. Compared with nonlinear model-predictive control, the measured implementation uses less CPU but has higher cost, settling time, and cart travel. The results support a conditional adaptive stabilization mechanism. 

# 1 Introduction 

Control-Lyapunov functions (CLFs) convert the stabilization problem into feedback selection once an appropriate state function is available. The classical universal construction of Sontag [1] makes this dependence explicit. The difficult part is often finding a suitable CLF, particularly when a locally stabilizing quadratic function does not describe the desired large excursion. Adaptive CLFs [2] address nonlinear stabilization with unknown plant parameters through a different structured design problem. The critic-as-Lyapunov-function (CALF) viewpoint uses a learned or adjusted critic as the stability object. Earlier work develops reinforcement-learning schemes with Lyapunov constraints [3], an actor-critic framework with environment stability guarantees [4], and a model-free CALF agent incorporating a nominal stabilizer [5]. The present construction is explicitly model-based : the known vector fields enter the critic derivative, and the sample-and-hold implementation predicts the next state. The online parameters describe a critic and not an estimate of unknown masses or other plant coefficients. No optimal value representation, Bellman equality, or ideal parameter vector is assumed here. No nominal stabilizers are assumed known either. The key observation is simple but operationally important. For a smooth critic V (x, θ ), the term ∇θ V ⊤ ˙θ

appears in its total derivative. One can therefore adapt the critic to meet a decay inequality while the state evolves. A projection that only preserves parameter bounds is insufficient: the same projection must also respect this particular derivative direction. This yields a general-purpose formula with a state-dependent feasibility condition, rather than an unconditional stabilizer for every stabilizable system. The contributions are as follows: (i) a bounded destination-projection formulation with an explicit feasibility budget and conditional state-stability proof; (ii) an exact-flow sampled counterpart whose endpoint

## 1.1 Related work and positioning 

Continuous-time online critic adaptation has a substantial history in adaptive dynamic programming. Vamvoudakis and Lewis [6] develop simultaneous actor and critic updates to approximate the infinite-horizon Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation, with excitation conditions for critic convergence. Dierks and Jagannathan [7] use a single online approximator for the value function and associated control, establishing uniform ultimate boundedness in the presence of approximation errors. These results establish precedents for continuous learning during control and for obtaining feedback from a critic. Our stabilization condition is imposed directly on the current critic’s total derivative, without requiring convergence to an optimal value representation. Parameter-dependent Lyapunov functions and adaptation laws are also established in adaptive control [2]. Lopez and Slotine [8] explicitly account for the parameter-derivative term and dynamically scale adaptation to handle estimation transients, starting from a family of CLFs for uncertain plant models. More directly, Sinaei and Ebeigbe [9] modify a parameter adaptation law through a minimally invasive real-time QP with robust adaptive CLF and barrier constraints for unknown dynamics. Thus filtering an adaptation law through Lyapunov constraints already has published precedents. In the present construction, the parameters are freely chosen critic coefficients for a known plant, and a frozen critic need only satisfy uniform positivity; its decrease is enforced using bounded parameter motion whenever the joint constraints are feasible. Lyapunov-constrained reinforcement learning addresses several different stability or safety notions. Chow et al. [10] construct Lyapunov constraints for policies in constrained Markov decision processes with expected cumulative-cost limits. Han et al. [11] learn a Lyapunov critic and actor using a data-based stability condition for stochastic systems. Khaniki et al. [12] project finite policy-parameter updates using a linearized expected Lyapunov decrement, while updating the Lyapunov-network parameters separately by gradient descent. Here the hard continuous-time constraint acts on the critic’s own parameter velocity through the exact chain-rule term ∇θ V ⊤ ˙θ, evaluated with the actually applied input. A complementary line jointly synthesizes feedback laws and Lyapunov certificates before deploying the resulting controller. Neural Lyapunov Control [13] combines learning with counterexample search and formal verification, while Neural Lyapunov Redesign [14] alternates certificate estimation and policy improvement to enlarge the region of attraction. Recent state and output feedback synthesis [15] and continuous-time Zubov-based training [16] use scalable neural-network verification to certify regions for the synthesized controllers. Our critic continues to evolve along the physical trajectory; the reported large-excursion cart-pole evidence is numerical, supplemented by a separate local certificate. The present study therefore supplies a different implementation and guarantee scope from formally verified controller synthesis. Finally, combining Lyapunov decrease and set invariance in a QP is standard CLF/CBF methodology [17]. For the augmented state ( x, θ ), one can interpret v = ˙θ as a virtual control input and {0} × Θ as the target set. This interpretation identifies the established optimization machinery underlying our construction. The specific contribution is the bounded destination constraint on critic motion, its exact pointwise compensation budget and persistent-feasibility boundary, together with the convex sampled endpoint formulation and reproducible case study.

## 2.3 Why feasibility is not automatic 

Consider ˙ x = x + u, V (x, θ ) = θx 2, Θ = [0 .1, 1], and u = −∂xV = −2θx . The interior parameter θ = 0 .75 stabilizes the plant with ˙V = −0.75 x2. Nevertheless, at θ = 0 .1, the derivative is 0 .16 x2 + x2v. Every invariant-box velocity satisfies v ≥ 0 there, making strict decay impossible for x̸ = 0. Thus even a uniformly positive family containing a stabilizing member need not remain feasible from all admissible initial parameters. Generally speaking, feasibility can be achieved for suitably chosen critic models. Sequential projection also fails in general. At a corner of R2+, the decay halfspace v1 + v2 ≤ 1 contains (−1, 2), but its projection onto the positive orthant is (0 , 2), outside the halfspace. This explains why an invariance projection cannot simply be appended to an otherwise successful adaptation formula.

# 5 Numerical Study 

## 5.1 Protocol and implementation 

The selected controller was frozen before the primary 300-state draw. Two hundred starts have independent uniform coordinates in [ −1, 1] × [−2, 2] × [−π, π ] × [−4, 4], using seed 20261024. One hundred near-hanging starts use independent uniforms x ∈ [−0.3, 0.3], v ∈ [−0.5, 0.5], ϑ ∈ π + [ −0.3, 0.3], ω ∈ [−0.7, 0.7], seed 20261025. The preceding twenty-start development screen compared curvature time constants 0 , 1, 2, 4 ms. Selection required all development paths to settle with clean numerical decay, a median paired variation ratio at most 0 .75, and a cost ratio at most 1 .1; eligible settings were ranked by variation, then cost, then time constant before drawing the fresh validation states. The initialization rule, model, force limit, and remaining coefficients were then held fixed. Simulations use Regelum 0.3.15 [19], with CasADi [20] CVODES plant integration at relative/absolute tolerances 10 −10 /10 −12 for the sampled controller. The continuous augmented state–parameter flow is integrated independently by DOP853 with 10 −7/10 −9 tolerances and maximum step 0 .02 s. The horizon is 45 s. A path is settled when its last two seconds remain within |s| < (0 .1, 0.2, 0.05 , 0.1); settling time is the earliest entry remaining inside through the horizon, with at least two seconds left. The held implementation retains the instantaneous halfspace and adds (13). At fixed y, the endpoint expression is quadratic in L+ and exponential in η+, hence convex. A conic formulation uses second-order and exponential cones and Clarabel [21]; rejected numerical candidates may be refined by SLSQP on the same convex constraints. A final segment repair toward a verified feasible witness is allowed only after checking the original constraints. This is not a different physical controller, and a repaired answer is not asserted to be the exact minimum-distance optimizer. Neither refinement nor repair can make a genuinely empty feasible set nonempty. Numerical success requires full-horizon completion, settling, and both the instantaneous residual and normalized endpoint residual at most 10 −6. Parameter-box and actual endpoint checks are retained. These finite tolerances are audit criteria, not replacements for the zero-tolerance inequalities in the theorems. Independent audits reintegrate recorded held inputs with DOP853 at 10 −12 /10 −14 . No audited interval with a genuinely positive reproduced violation is relabeled as numerical noise.

## 5.3 Audits, computation, and a negative result 

Thirty-two primary full replays reproduce their archived arrays exactly. Independent reintegration checks 720000 held intervals: all 16 selected candidate paths pass and 15 of 16 reference paths pass, reproducing the known reference failure. Maximum state disagreement is below 7 .03 × 10 −9. The audits include selected rather than all primary paths; they do not turn the full sampled batch into a validated enclosure. All 500 primary candidate numerical paths enter the very small local ellipsoid (23) and stay inside at subsequent logged nodes. Numerical entry is not an exact-flow proof that swing-up reaches that set. A quiet single-core benchmark replays six matched initial states for each controller with three sequential repeats. The median paired total CPU ratio NMPC/CALF is 1.6703. The respective median CPU rates are 0.3819 and 0.2277 s per simulated second. This measures different controllers at their respective 100 ms and 2 ms update periods, not equal-cost solves. Median steady deadline-miss fractions are 0.1114% and 0.7023%; worst steady calls are 431.43 and 4.93 ms. The simulated plant does not include these computation delays. Neither hard-real-time operation nor a worst-case execution-time bound is established. The longer-hold extension is important counterevidence. A 4 ms hold with a 4 ms curvature penalty passed all 20 reused development starts but failed the next draw: one sampled path had two reproduced endpoint violations despite physically settling, and one continuous path lost feasibility. The maximum sampled residual was 140.58064, not a roundoff-sized excursion. This candidate was not promoted. Changing the penalty changes the feedback, so this is not a discretization-convergence test of one fixed continuous law. 

# 6 Discussion and Conclusion 

The results demonstrate that an adaptive positive critic can support force-limited swing-up and stabilization without encoding mechanical-energy error, learning from replay data, or switching to a separate local controller. The local Riccati anchor and deterministic initialization remain meaningful prior structure; the method is neither model-free nor independent of design choices. Its role is to supply an online mechanism for enforcing a specified critic decrease, not to establish value-function optimality. The exact pointwise test identifies the compensation available from bounded parameter motion. It does not prove that the available compensation will persist along the future path. # C Reproducibility and Claim Boundaries 

The ancillary material contains the frozen cart-pole configuration, all compact primary and second-draw outcome tables, the supplementary continuous evaluation, matched NMPC and paired-reference tables, independent audit summaries, timing summaries, and exact local-certificate constants. The figure script regenerates the two figures from those data alone. A separate checker recomputes the exact local constants using the archived binary matrix, without dependence on a particular floating-point Riccati solver. The representative trajectory is a compact reduction of a full audited replay; its bin maxima preserve all positive residual excursions. Source hashes identify the underlying archived data. No double-pendulum or other plant result is used in this preprint. There are 600 distinct sampled initial conditions, not 1000 independent states obtained by adding 400 continuous evaluations. The matched NMPC table repeats 100 of the primary states, and replay/audit records repeat selected paths. A zero observed failure count is not a proof of a region or a probability-one guarantee. The local exact-arithmetic calculation concerns an ideal nominal controller and cannot be promoted to a machine-arithmetic or validated full-swing-up certificate.
